How to Support Your Team During a Layoff EN

Practical tips to lead with empathy and care through difficult times

There are certain signs in companies that make you start to worry, such as hiring freezes, salary increases being postponed, or even Kit-Kats no longer being restocked in the office kitchen.

I hope none of these examples sound familiar to you and that you're having a great professional experience. Unfortunately, that wasn't the case for me. I was impacted by a layoff, along with my team, after many months of fearing the worst.

In her post, The 5 Stages of Grief in Tech Layoffs, my friend Silvia perfectly describes the emotional burden of a layoff. It's a must-read for anyone going through this difficult situation and seeking resilience.

In this post, I want to share a few tips that helped me support my team during those uncertain months.

💡 Tips for Supporting Your Team During a Layoff

  1. Be kind. You're in the same difficult situation as your team. It's important they see that you share their frustration and discontent. Showing them you're on their side will foster a stronger connection.
  2. Be transparent. Help prevent the spread of misinformation by sharing only accurate information from trusted sources.
  3. Provide regular updates. When there is no major change, regular updates can reassure the team that you are working to fix these challenges.
  4. Prioritize one-on-one meetings. Your team members need you now more than ever. They'll have countless questions, and you'll need to be available to answer them quickly.
  5. Adjust workloads thoughtfully. Reduce the workload when possible to prevent burnout and ensure the team can perform effectively without feeling overwhelmed.
  6. Forget conventional meetings. The team is likely to see no value in sticking to the established plan, so focus on adapting to their needs and prioritizing what matters most.
  7. Provide career support. Provide assistance such as resume reviews, networking tips and referrals. Also, provide access to mental health resources or career coaching to fully support team members.
  8. Encourage skill-building opportunities. Help the team see the situation as a chance to explore new areas of development or continue acquiring new skills.

Managing your team during redundancy is challenging. Your presence, understanding, and leadership are absolutely crucial during this time.

❤️‍🩹 Take care of yourself and your team throughout this entire process.
